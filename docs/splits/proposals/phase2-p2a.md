## 1. Method (one implementation: `tools/splits/dataattach.py`)

* The runs: every maximal stretch of map symbols no unit of the phase 1 candidate covers, in the lane's sections (the 5 `.data`, 2 `.bss`, 5 `.sbss`,
  1 `.sdata2`, 2 `.rodata` runs of `phase1-reconcile.md` section 5, plus the start-of-link-order `.sdata` run, plus the 3 provisional by-reader
  pieces of `mh3_pad`). A run's candidates are the units from the last owner before it to the first owner after it in link order.
* Votes (decoded from the retail text with `splitcheck`'s own decoder): **D** = units whose text reads the symbol (pool literals by a load only);
  **P** = units that own it by reference (a data object whose words point at it and that a unit reads, an owned table that points at it, the
  units of a vtable's slots). P is used in `.data`/`.rodata` only (section 2).
* A monotone dynamic program places the run's symbols among the candidates (cost 3 for a symbol placed away from all its D readers, 1 for P), twice,
  ties to the earliest and to the latest candidate. A symbol both give the same unit is **decided** (signal 8: a unit's symbols are contiguous and the
  runs are in link order); a symbol they disagree on is **undecided** and is listed in `unowned_data` with its candidates, never given to a guess.
* Grade of a decided segment (edges = its first and last symbol): **strong** both edges read by the unit alone or abutting a range it already owns
  (`registered_edge`) and at least two sole reads; **medium** both edges carry a vote; else guess (a deferral). A strong data-order seam wholly inside caps it at
  medium. A segment other units read or point at more often than the unit's own votes (10 or more) is **contradicted**: deferred, not attached.
* `python tools/splits/dataattach.py --lane p2a --explain "<unit>" --section <sec>` prints a unit's segment with every symbol's class and votes (the
  `reproduce` of each entry); `--show-run <addr>` prints a run's segments, intervals and reader clusters.

## 2. How reliable is the placement (backtest, `dataattach.py --backtest`)

Every registered data symbol (3,745 over six sections) is placed with its ownership hidden among **all** the units that own that section (97 for `.data`; a far
harder problem than a run's two or three candidates), the owned-table votes left out because they are the ownership:

| section | symbols | decided | right | wrong |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `.sdata2` | 443 | 287 | 287 | 0 |
| `.data` | 2,794 | 2,662 | 2,660 | 2 |
| `.bss` | 151 | 138 | 138 | 0 |
| `.sbss` | 129 | 128 | 128 | 0 |
| `.sdata` | 154 | 56 | 56 | 0 |
| `.rodata` | 74 | 73 | 73 | 0 |
| all | 3,745 | 3,344 | 3,342 | 2 |

With the P votes also in `.sdata` it is 41 of 56 (`--p-sections .data,.rodata,.sdata,...`): a `.sdata` symbol owned by reference is often another unit's extern, so P counts
for `.data`/`.rodata` only (an owned `.data` table points at a symbol of its own unit 1,335 of 1,335 times, a `.data` -> `.bss` pointer 42 of 58).
The two wrong `.data` symbols are sole-read symbols placed with the wrong unit (a read does not make the reader the definer). A decided symbol is therefore a placement, not a proof;
what the backtest cannot see is a run whose bounding registered ranges are wrong (section 5, run 2).

## 7. Format and checker changes (their own commits, `tools/splitcheck: ...`)

* `200f64572` the phase 2 fields: `attach` (a unit of the candidate, data ranges, grade/evidence/reproduce, `guess` never applied; naming a recut proposal unit replaces the by-reader assignment of the run) and `unowned_data`, a file may have no
  `units`; 8 selftests; `docs/splits-program.md`.
* `ac000f7aa` **fix**: `check_pool` sorted the first-use list of `.sdata` strings and `.sdata2` literals of a unit together by address, so a unit claiming both reported an inversion that is two pools; the order is now judged per section (selftest fails without the fix).
* `306bfd871` units an `attach` gives data to are listed with the proposal units and their FAILs are the proposal's.
* `3387fb50a` **semantics**: a strong V->S row asserts a boundary in `[first string, next vtable)`; `data-order` failed whenever the first string was inside the unit, although the unit's own end can be that boundary. It fails now only when the next vtable group is
  also inside the unit. Baseline FAIL 3 -> 0 (`nw_resource`, `Network/NetworkSessionManager`, `Network/fn_8041A87C` each end inside their gap), 3 selftests (two fail without the fix). The change makes the invariant weaker for a registered unit that ends inside a gap: say so if the owner prefers the old reading.

## 8. Tool gaps and residuals

1. **No way to place a data-only unit.** A proposal unit without `.text` is inserted at the END of the candidate (`render` anchors a unit by its first `.text` start), so the stage block (section 5, item 1) cannot be proposed as a data-only TU between `fn_80047398` and `draw_shape`. Needs a `after: <unit>` anchor (additive).
2. **`pool` on `.sdata` strings** is not reliable for TUs with inline functions (section 6); the unit-level `pool` verdict mixes literal-order evidence and string-order evidence.
3. **A big map symbol is one object**: `jumptable_8059FE90` (0x224 B) collects the decoded displacements of four units; `splitcheck`'s `jumptable` picks an arbitrary reader (the unit named differs from run to run: set order).
4. **Reads through pointers are invisible**: a `.bss` buffer reached through a global pointer variable has no D vote; the `.bss` intervals of section 5 are exactly those.
5. The DP assumes the registered ranges are right (they bound the runs): run 2 shows what happens when the readers say they are not; an automatic cross-check of every registered range against its readers is the next tool (`dataattach.py --backtest` is its precision measurement, not yet its audit).
6. A recut registered unit's data cannot be "left whole": an `attach` naming the mover replaces the by-reader result for the run it covers, nothing expresses "the mover gets nothing".
7. `unowned_data` entries are rendered nowhere but the count line; a later phase should feed them to `tudiscover`/`dataclaim`.
